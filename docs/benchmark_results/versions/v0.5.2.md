## Quantizing the MTP drafter, Qwen 3.8 27B on M5 Max

Quantizing the Qwen3.8 drafter to 4-bit cut draft_block cost about 3.9x and left verify-forward time unchanged on M5 Max. Two repetitions at two lengths gave mixed-sign small acceptance changes and roughly 1.5x over the separately measured classic rate. Preserve the experiment's exactness-override context; it is not automatically a shipping exact-MTP claim.

## MTP drafter step profile, Qwen 3.8 27B on M1 Ultra

On M1 Ultra, drafter layers accounted for 68.2% of profiled step cost, the borrowed LM head 19.7%, and readback 5.8%. Quantization targets the largest component; removing host readback alone cannot recover the whole drafter cost. The 14.1 ms/round accept hook was larger than draft_block and outside the original optimization phases. One host/pair does not establish general costs.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/mtp-drafter-step-profile-m1ultra-2026-08-17.md); [#1185](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/1185), [#1182](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/1182).

## MTP drafter step profile, Qwen 3.8 27B on M5 Max

M5 Max cut verify time to 35.24 ms/round but left draft_block around 10.67 ms, essentially flat against M1 Ultra; the accept hook added 10.60 ms. Drafter-side work thus became 36.3% of a 58.5 ms round. The exactness gate failed and MLXCEL_MTP_ALLOW_INEXACT=1 was required: these are explicitly non-shipping profiling numbers.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/mtp-drafter-step-profile-m5max-2026-08-17.md); [#1185](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/1185), [#1182](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/1182), [#1189](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/1189).

## Qwen3.8-27B MTP speculative decoding — M1 Ultra, 2026-08-16

Qwen3.8 MTP on M1 Ultra was checked through production CLI/server paths with chain-parity controls. Same-prompt before/after parity-kernel runs had walls inside noise and near-tie acceptance reshuffling; a separately quoted 0.274-acceptance baseline used different prompts and was not attributable. Token-level correctness and boundary detokenization are separate observations.

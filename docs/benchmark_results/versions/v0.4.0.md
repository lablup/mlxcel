# Benchmark decision archive — v0.4.0

[Benchmark index](../README.md)

This groups records by the first stable release tag containing the report, verified from Git trees, not by filename dates. It is **not** a benchmark of that release binary. Each linked frozen record preserves the actual measured commits (including dirty/branch builds), hardware, model, flags, repetitions, dispersion, attribution and unmeasured cells. Later corrections present at the consolidation snapshot are included; decisions describe the experiment, not necessarily today's defaults. `unreleased` means no stable tag yet contains the report.

<a id="cache-clear-cadence-gb10-2026-07-08"></a>

## CUDA cache-clear cadence + `MLXCEL_CACHE_LIMIT` (issue #627, GB10)

Periodic CUDA cache clearing showed no throughput or memory benefit on GB10, including the 16k soak. The CUDA interval became 0; Metal/CPU stayed at 256 and were not remeasured. This is removal of dead work plus an operator cache-limit knob, not a speedup claim.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/cache-clear-cadence-gb10-2026-07-08.md); [#627](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/627), [#688](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/688).

<a id="cuda-kv-quant-modes-gb10-2026-07-10"></a>

## Quantized KV cache modes on CUDA (GB10) status matrix

All six KV cache modes ran on GB10 without a Metal-only-kernel abort because unsupported kernels fall back to MLX graphs. The genuine defect was the Int8 scale floor of 1.0; replacing only zero scales restored byte-identical greedy output against FP16 on the validation prompt. Batched 4-bit cache quantization was not affected; functional support does not imply every mode is faster.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/cuda-kv-quant-modes-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#635](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/635), [#623](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/623).

<a id="hybrid-ssm-decode-cuda-kernel-gb10-2026-07-10"></a>

## Hybrid-SSM decode: CUDA port of the fused SSM update kernel (issue #631, GB10)

The fused CUDA SSM update raised GB10 decode from 62.8 to 281.7 tok/s on Granite 350M, 38.9 to 101.8 on Granite tiny and 41.5 to 88.1 on Nemotron-H. The Mamba2 control remained unchanged, and greedy parity was checked. These measured hybrid-family gains do not generalize to every dtype/checkpoint; the f32 Plamo row remained far below its Metal reference.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/hybrid-ssm-decode-cuda-kernel-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#631](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/631).

<a id="longprompt-prefill-gb10"></a>

## Long-prompt prefill benchmark ladder (epic #623 #624)

The GB10 512–32768-token ladder demonstrated why short prompts cannot characterize prefill: dense 7–8B rows reached 1600–3500 tok/s while MoE rows were much slower and several long-context cells aborted or ran out of memory. Treat failed/OOM cells as such, not throughput zero; the original CSV in benchmarks/ remains the historical baseline.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/longprompt-prefill-gb10.md); [#623](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/623), [#624](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/624).

<a id="mlx-pin-upgrade-2026-07-03"></a>

## MLX Pin Upgrade 2026-07-03: a6ec712 -> e9463bb (GB10 Re-baseline)

The MLX pin rebaseline separated actual regressions from unsupported checkpoint formats. The historical 0.89 tok/s ModelOpt NVFP4 row was degenerate output, not a valid performance baseline; the genuine native-NVFP4 scale parsing defect was separate. MoE prefill still bypassed upstream's JIT gather path and required its own follow-up. The frozen source preserves the per-outlier verdicts.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/mlx-pin-upgrade-2026-07-03.md); [#625](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/625), [#623](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/623), [#3723](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/3723), [#3706](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/3706), [#3576](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/3576).

<a id="moe-prefill-grouped-gemm-gb10-2026-07-10"></a>

## MoE prefill: sorted GatherQMM via dequant + CUTLASS grouped GEMM (issue #629, GB10)

Sorted MoE prefill through dequantization plus grouped GEMM improved GB10 pp2048 throughput 3.6–40.2x across the measured affine/MXFP4 models (Mixtral 14.2 to 570.9 tok/s). Decode remained close to baseline. These large prefill gains are shape-specific and must not be reused as decode or serving speedups.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/moe-prefill-grouped-gemm-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#629](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/629), [#3706](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/3706), [#625](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/625).

## ModelOpt NVFP4 direct transcode benchmark (issue #693)

Direct ModelOpt NVFP4 transcode avoids the dense-f16 requantization detour. Subsequent scaled-MLP evidence distinguishes single-token compiled decode from multi-token prefill: routing prefill into the eager scale fold is not the same optimization. Keep the archived per-shape/load comparisons and original CSVs; do not conflate load-time, prefill and decode gains.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/nvfp4-direct-transcode-gb10-2026-07-08.md); [#693](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/693), [#692](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/692).

## ModelOpt NVFP4 CUDA repack benchmark (issue #630)

The repaired loader accepts the specific ModelOpt NVFP4 triplet metadata and repacks it for CUDA. Gemma 4 31B short-prompt native decode measured 5.24 tok/s versus 4.48 for the affine stopgap; the old 0.89 row produced invalid output and is not a clean speedup baseline. Prompt/output counts differ across historical rows.

<a id="nvfp4-native-metal-m1ultra-2026-07-09"></a>

## ModelOpt NVFP4 native Metal A/B benchmark (issue #694)

Native NVFP4 on M1 Ultra improved short-prompt load wall time from 281.78 to 83.78 seconds and decode from 4.76 to 12.70 tok/s. But pp2048 prefill fell from 124.58 to 115.61 tok/s, failing the required prefill-improvement gate. This experiment alone did not justify a default switch; the later prefill recovery record is separate.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/nvfp4-native-metal-m1ultra-2026-07-09.md); [#694](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/694), [#693](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/693).

## Native NVFP4 prefill recovery and default switch on M1 Ultra

The later native-NVFP4 Metal work made direct transcode the default with an explicit dense-repack escape hatch. A focused scaled-MLP A/B at pp2048 was essentially tied (116.10 versus 116.11 tok/s) and pp8192 likewise close, so do not attribute a new gain to that graph alone. The source separates the pin, load-path and activation changes from the earlier failed gate.

## Paged-attention decode: CUDA port of the fused kernel (issue #634, GB10)

Port fused paged decode to CUDA rather than carrying over Metal's batch/context ceiling unmeasured. GB10 correctness and kernel comparisons support native single-slab dispatch; the CUDA benefit grows with context. The record distinguishes the kernel A/B from the end-to-end sanity check and does not establish identical dispatch policy for other backends.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/paged-attention-cuda-port-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#634](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/634), [#720](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/720).

## sm_120/121 quantized GEMM tile tuning for Blackwell (issue #637, GB10)

The sm_120/121 tile change improved large-M prefill about 32% and left M=1 qmv decode untouched. It did not solve small-M batching; that gap was subsequently addressed by multirow qmv. Keep the two shape regimes separate when explaining the default or citing the result.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/qmm-sm121-tile-tuning-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#637](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/637), [#725](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/725).

<a id="qmv-multirow-gb10-2026-07-11"></a>

## Multirow qmv: amortizing small-M quantized matmul on GB10 (CUDA), 2026-07-11

Multirow qmv amortizes shared affine weights for CUDA small-M decode/verify instead of rereading them per row. The measured GB10 MTP pairing subsequently reached 1.31x/1.46x/1.41x at widths 2/4/8 with unchanged acceptance. Non-affine fp_qmv, per-expert gather and the exact M*B=8 GEMM boundary were separate unmeasured tuning questions.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/qmv-multirow-gb10-2026-07-11.md); [#725](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/725), [#638](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/638).

## Scheduler decode lookahead pipelining acceptance (issue #632, GB10)

Lookahead met the dense-backend GB10 acceptance on Qwen2.5 0.5B and Llama 8B; paged attention and B=4 quantized matmul were separate limits. The July 11 multirow-qmv update supersedes the old B=4 caveat. MLXCEL_FORCE_SYNC=1 remains the diagnostic rollback, not the measured preferred default.

## Serving-throughput defaults: parallel decode, batched prefill, prompt cache (issue #628, M1 Ultra)

M1 Ultra measurements supported parallelism/batching/cache defaults, with 1.90x aggregate throughput and roughly 17x TTFT-under-load improvement at four clients, no measured singleton regression and no OOM. Preserve explicit opt-outs and the host/workload restriction; this is not a CUDA default measurement.

<a id="single-dtype-decode-astype-gb10-2026-07-10"></a>

## Single-dtype decode graph: per-token AsType inventory on CUDA (GB10)

CUDA f16 normalization remains opt-in: quantized models are unaffected and bf16-to-f16 throughput differences were inside noise on the measured dense model. AsType graph inventory is diagnostic, not proof that every conversion is wasted work. Keep quantized scales/biases intact and do not generalize Metal precision policy to CUDA.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/single-dtype-decode-astype-gb10-2026-07-10.md); [#636](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/636).

<a id="speculative-acceptance-m5max-2026-07-11"></a>

## Gemma 4 MTP acceptance-rate cross-check on Apple Silicon (M5 Max), 2026-07-11

The same short prompt gave the same 35.0% Gemma 4 MTP acceptance on M5 Max and GB10, refuting a CUDA-specific acceptance defect. Longer prompts gave 52–88%. The backend difference was verify-round cost; checkpoint and workload are the acceptance levers, not an unsupported numerical fix.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/speculative-acceptance-m5max-2026-07-11.md); [#638](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/638), [#733](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/733), [#737](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/737), [#725](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/725).

The original GB10 speculative-pairing loss was superseded by multirow qmv: the same pairing reached 1.31x, 1.46x and 1.41x at widths 2/4/8 with unchanged acceptance. The later Apple cross-check refuted a backend acceptance gap. Keep the historical cause as verify cost, not low acceptance attributed to CUDA.

## Fused decode-MoE kernel: design and roadmap (#268)

The original fused-MoE design targeted GPU idle gaps around expert matmuls, not hidden Rust FFI time or tiny router/combine operations. A correctness-first single-threadgroup prototype was about 88x slower despite byte-identical output. Retain the design's measured rejection of that geometry and staged correctness/real-checkpoint gates; the frozen source preserves the full historical roadmap, not a current implementation plan.

[Full record and reproduction](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/blob/2dc8ed47b30dc204d892328098e8cc34ec02ddc1/docs/benchmark_results/fused-moe-decode-kernel-design.md); [#268](https://github.com/lablup/mlxcel/issues/268).

Related evidence / subsequent correction: [fused-moe-dff-crossover-gb10-2026-11-11.md](../fused-moe-dff-crossover-gb10-2026-11-11.md) re-measures the CUDA Dff bound that this record's 2026-07 addenda set and confirmed. The bound stays at 8192, but the llama-4-scout row those addenda read as break-even at 8192 never ran the fused kernel.

## MoE decode performance gap investigation (mlxcel vs mlx-lm)

The historical MoE decode gap tracked extra small operations around expert computation while GPU time dominated and Rust graph-build time was hidden. Dense Llama/Granite were at parity; Qwen3-MoE and dots1 were much lower. Use the record as attribution motivating expert-path work, not a current cross-runtime ranking or evidence that every small fusion pays.

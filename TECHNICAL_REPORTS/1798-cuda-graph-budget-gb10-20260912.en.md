# Raising MLX's CUDA graph budgets on GB10: measured per family, shipped for one

Issue #1798. Host GB10 (sm_121, DGX Spark), MLX pin `81ba1c6a`, CUDA release build, toolchain 1.97.1, source tree `d2b60c8e` for the measurement binary. Full measurement record, with raw per-run data and the harness under `data/`: `docs/benchmark_results/cuda-graph-budget-gb10-2026-09-12.md`.

## Background

#1782's control sweep found that `MLX_MAX_OPS_PER_BUFFER=100` gave classic Qwen 3.5 decode about +3% on GB10, one model and one workload, and #1798 was filed at low priority to find out whether that generalized. Before the work started, #1799's controls on Laguna changed the premise: at GB10's default budgets, CUDA graph capture on that MoE was a net loss (graphs off beat the default by 12%) and only raising both budgets made capture worth having (+16%), while on Qwen 3.5 the sign of capture was the opposite. The two knobs also masked each other. So the question became whether any default could be justified, what it would have to be gated on, and what it costs in instantiation and memory.

## The mechanism, verified in the pinned source

MLX commits a captured CUDA graph after any op that pushes either of two counters past a per-architecture budget (`needs_commit`, `mlx/backend/cuda/device.cpp:458-460`, from `eval.cpp:73`). `get_graph_limits` (`device.cpp:181-202`) gives compute capability 12.1 20 ops and 25 "MB", the tightest byte budget in its table, against 100 and 1000 for consumer Blackwell, H100 and B200. The byte counter sums `array::data_size()` over the graph's inputs (`device.cpp:228`), and `data_size` is an element count, not bytes (`mlx/array.h:346`). So the budget is 26.2M input elements, and any op reading an array larger than that commits its own graph: each stacked 256-expert NVFP4 projection on Laguna (33.5M words, about 120 `gather_qmm` per token), and every 4-bit lm_head or tied embedding above the cap (Qwen 3.5 4B 79.5M, Llama 3.1 8B 65.7M). An nsys pair confirmed the count: a Laguna token at the defaults is 200 committed graphs of under 12 nodes each; with both budgets raised it is 24. Kernel launches per token are identical (2,365), host-side CUDA API time is flat (17 ms per token), and the wall time falls 5.6 ms: the saving is device-side, about 30 us of launch latency and inter-graph event wait per boundary removed. That is a reduction in the total, not a phase shift; the DFlash phase split at block 4 shows the same thing (drafter host build 12.9 to 7.6 ms, device sync flat, round wall 65.6 to 60.1 ms).

## What the measurement said

Same binary on every arm, environment variables as the only difference, GPU held under the lock, host idle (load gated before each phase, compiler processes gated before each run after a CI job overlapped one phase, load and CI state recorded per run), `MLX_ENABLE_TF32=0`, n = 3 per arm with min and max, seven checkpoints, four workloads.

The sign of raising both budgets to MLX's own Blackwell row (100 ops, 1000 "MB") is a property of the model family and the workload, not of the device. Single-stream decode: Laguna +17%, qwen3-30b-a3b +21%, qwen3.5-35b-a3b +22%, all with disjoint ranges; Qwen 3.5 4B dense +4%; gemma-4-26b-a4b flat; gpt-oss-20b -7% with +7 GB of peak memory; Llama 3.1 8B -9%, disjoint. Each knob alone is inert where the other binds (Laguna: op budget +1%, byte budget +11%, both +17%; Llama: -0.2%, -0.1%, both -9%), which is the masking effect #1799 predicted and the reason single-knob evaluations undercount. No config-level shape rule separates the winners from the losers: gpt-oss's expert stack is over the byte cap and it loses, qwen3-30b-a3b's is under and it wins. Llama's loss is graph re-instantiation: with 100-node graphs spanning several layers, the attention op's geometry change every few tokens invalidates a whole 100-node graph (0.14 instantiations per token at 8 ms each) instead of a 12-node one, and the host starts stalling 11 ms per token on `cudaEventSynchronize`.

The costs asked for are measured. Instantiation is small where the budget helps (Laguna 0.34 ms per token, 16x under the gain; Qwen 3.5 none) and is the mechanism where it hurts. Memory is not small: a graph spanning five times as many ops keeps the intermediate arrays of all of them alive until it commits, +0.4 GB on a 112-token prompt and +7.0 GB on Laguna's 2048-token prefill chunk (22.7 to 29.8 GB), +5.2 GB on Qwen 3.5's. Long prefill itself barely moves with the budget (Laguna -1.5%, Qwen 3.5 +11% slower). Graphs off is not a candidate either: it beats the default on Laguna classic decode (+11%) but not the raised budgets, and costs 3 to 15% of classic decode on every other model.

Two findings outside this issue's decision are recorded for follow-up: CUDA graph capture more than doubles prefill time on every MoE measured (Laguna 7.5 s against 3.2 s for a 2048-token prompt with graphs off, qwen3-30b-a3b -64%, gemma-4-26b-a4b -65%), and on the Laguna DFlash arm graphs off beats every graphs-on arm because the drafter's host build halves.

Batched serving separated two families that single-stream had put together. Laguna's `supports_batching()` is false (`src/models/laguna.rs:650`), so its server rows are a serialized workload, and they gain (+8% at concurrency 1, disjoint; +4 to +6% at 4 and 8). The two Qwen MoE families do batch, and they are why the bar is every workload rather than decode alone. Measured at n = 3 after a reboot, qwen3-30b-a3b gains at concurrency 1 (+12.6%, disjoint) and does nothing at 4 or 8, while qwen3.5-35b-a3b, whose server path had never been measured, is flat at 1 and 4 and loses 10.7% at concurrency 8 with disjoint ranges against a +22.5% single-stream gain. Their long-prefill rows agree: prefill 14.4% and 8.0% slower, at +16.3 GB and +8.2 GB of peak memory. Neither joins the allowlist. Graphs off is the largest effect on qwen3-30b-a3b's batched rows (+32.4% at concurrency 8) and uniformly worse on qwen3.5-35b-a3b, so the sign of a capture-policy change does not carry even within one architecture family.

## The decision, and what it is gated on

A single global default is ruled out by disjoint ranges of opposite sign. A device-only default for sm_121 is ruled out the same way (Llama). The default that ships is an allowlist, the shape #353 used for the Metal knob: `apply_cuda_graph_budget_default` (`src/lib/mlxcel-core/src/cuda_graph_budget.rs`, re-exported through `hardware`) reads the checkpoint's `config.json` before any MLX op, and sets `MLX_MAX_OPS_PER_BUFFER=100` and `MLX_MAX_MB_PER_BUFFER=1000` only when the device is compute capability 12.1 and the `model_type` is in `GB10_RAISED_BUDGET_MODEL_TYPES` with a routed-expert count over 1. An operator-set value wins per variable. Every entry point that already applies the graph-cache and SDPA-cache defaults now passes the `-m` path it was given (`mlxcel generate`, `run`, `serve`, `embed`, `rerank`, `tune`, `mlxcel-server`, `mlxcel-bench-decode`, `mlxcel-speculative-bench`); a repo id not yet on disk, router mode and a command without a model apply nothing. The bar for listing a family is that every measured workload gains with n = 3 and disjoint ranges, and the memory cost is stated. The list is `["laguna"]`, and after this round of measurement it is still exactly that: the only two candidates for widening it were measured and both failed, one on the serving path and one on serving and prefill together.

The applied budget is observable: `mlxcel generate` prints a `CUDA graph budget: ... applied for model_type ... on sm_121` line beside its compute-capability line, `mlxcel-server` logs the same at info in its startup hardware record, and neither prints anything when nothing was applied.

That the shipped binaries reach the raised budget is measured, not inferred, because a policy compiled in but never called would look identical in every table above. Three arms on Laguna, same binary, n = 3: no environment at all, an operator-restored 20/25, and an explicit 100/1000. Through `mlxcel-bench-decode` the no-environment arm is 37.42 tok/s (37.22 to 37.71) and the explicit arm 37.50 (37.24 to 37.64), overlapping across their whole ranges, while the restored arm is 31.76 (31.09 to 32.31), disjoint from both, +17.8% for the shipped default. MLX peak memory separates the same way without involving the clock at all (22.03 GB against 21.66 GB). Through `mlxcel generate` it is 34.13 against 29.29, through `mlxcel-server` 29.03 against 27.70, disjoint in both. The dense checkpoint outside the allowlist shows the gate is the allowlist and not the device: its three arms overlap and sit on MLX's own numbers. The startup lines cover the env-wins contract in both halves, whole-variable (both set by the operator, nothing applied and nothing printed) and per-variable (only the op budget set, the policy fills in the byte budget alone).

## Blast radius

The change reaches every CUDA build, but the default fires only on compute capability 12.1 (DGX Spark, a 128 GB unified-memory machine, which bounds the memory cost) and only for the listed families. Measured on this host: Laguna (every workload), qwen3-30b-a3b and qwen3.5-35b-a3b (single-stream and TBD), Qwen 3.5 4B, Llama 3.1 8B, gpt-oss-20b, gemma-4-26b-a4b (single-stream and short prefill; the last four are left at MLX's defaults, so the default cannot regress them). Every other family on CUDA merely shares the code path and is unchanged. Volta (#1545) and every capability other than 12.1 are untouched by construction. Metal and Accelerate gates were not run: this host has neither.

## Follow-ups

- Capture overhead on MoE prefill (2x on every MoE measured with graphs off) and on the DFlash drafter build: a per-phase capture policy is a separate issue.
- An 8192-token prefill peak on Laguna to confirm the memory cost stays bounded per 2048-token chunk, and intermediate budgets (50 ops, or 100 "MB") that might keep the decode gain with less retention; both were queued and not run.
- An nsys pair on the batched path, to explain why the raised budgets stop helping as a batch fills. It is a mechanism question rather than a gate: the throughput rows already settle both Qwen families in the negative.
